# CPIC Clinical Guideline Corpus for Pharmacogenomics

## Guideline: DPYD and Fluoropyrimidines (5-Fluorouracil, Capecitabine, Tegafur)
Dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD, encoded by the DPYD gene) is the initial and rate-limiting enzyme in the catabolism of the fluoropyrimidine chemotherapy agents 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) and capecitabine. More than 80% of an administered dose of 5-FU is rapidly broken down by DPD into inactive 5,6-dihydrofluorouracil in the liver. Genetic variations in DPYD that eliminate or decrease DPD catalytic activity cause impaired drug clearance and prolonged systemic exposure to active cytotoxic metabolites, specifically fluorouridine triphosphate (FUTP) and fluorodeoxyuridine monophosphate (FdUMP). Patients carrying non-functional variants, such as DPYD *2A (rs3918290, c.1905+1G>A), DPYD *13 (rs55704559, c.1679T>G), or decreased-function variants (c.2846A>T, HapB3), have an activity score of 1.0 (intermediate metabolizer) or 0.0 (poor metabolizer). In intermediate metabolizers, starting doses must be decreased by 25% to 50% to prevent life-threatening grade 4-5 toxicities, including profound neutropenic sepsis, severe stomatitis, enteritis, and death. In poor metabolizers with complete deficiency, all fluoropyrimidines are strictly contraindicated.

## Guideline: Warfarin Pharmacogenetics (CYP2C9, VKORC1, CYP4F2)
Warfarin oral anticoagulant therapy requires precise individualized dosing due to its narrow therapeutic index and significant bleeding risk. S-warfarin is 3 to 5 times more potent than R-warfarin and is metabolized almost exclusively by cytochrome P450 2C9 (CYP2C9). Common alleles CYP2C9 *2 (rs1799853) and CYP2C9 *3 (rs1057910) encode enzymes with decreased clearance, necessitating significant dose reductions to avoid supratherapeutic INR and hemorrhage. The pharmacological target of warfarin is Vitamin K Epoxide Reductase Complex Subunit 1 (VKORC1). The promoter variant VKORC1 -1639G>A (rs9923231) reduces VKORC1 transcriptional activity and protein expression, conferring heightened pharmacodynamic sensitivity to warfarin. Additionally, CYP4F2 (*3, rs2108622) oxidizes vitamin K1 to hydroxyvitamin K1, regulating the hepatic pool of vitamin K; carrying the *3 allele decreases enzyme activity, increases baseline vitamin K1, and moderately raises the required warfarin dose. Combined algorithm-based dosing (IWPC) incorporating all three genes provides superior dosing accuracy compared to single-gene or empirical clinical estimates.

## Guideline: SLCO1B1 and Simvastatin-Induced Myopathy
Solute Carrier Organic Anion Transporter Family Member 1B1 (SLCO1B1), located on chromosome 12p12, encodes the hepatic influx transporter OATP1B1. OATP1B1 mediates the active sinusoidal uptake of statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors) from portal blood into hepatocytes, the primary site of cholesterol synthesis and statin efficacy. The non-synonymous variant SLCO1B1 *5 (c.521T>C, rs4149056) leads to altered protein sorting and diminished cellular membrane localization of the transporter. In individuals with decreased (heterozygous *1/*5) or poor (homozygous *5/*5) transporter function, hepatic uptake is significantly compromised, producing higher systemic plasma concentrations of simvastatin acid (up to 220% higher AUC per *5 allele). Elevated systemic exposure directly triggers mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress in myocytes, predisposing patients to statin-associated muscle symptoms, acute myopathy, and life-threatening rhabdomyolysis. CPIC recommends avoiding high-dose simvastatin or switching to alternative statins like pravastatin or rosuvastatin that have less reliance on SLCO1B1.

## Guideline: CYP2C19 and Antiplatelet Therapy (Clopidogrel)
Clopidogrel is an oral thienopyridine prodrug extensively prescribed to reduce atherothrombotic events and stent thrombosis in patients presenting with acute coronary syndromes (ACS) or undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). Clopidogrel requires hepatic bioactivation via a two-step cytochrome P450 enzymatic cascade, in which CYP2C19 plays a critical role in generating the active thiol metabolite that selectively and irreversibly inhibits the platelet P2Y12 ADP receptor. Individuals carrying loss-of-function alleles CYP2C19 *2 (rs4244285) or CYP2C19 *3 (rs4986893) produce significantly lower levels of the active metabolite, resulting in subtherapeutic platelet inhibition, high on-treatment platelet reactivity, and markedly elevated rates of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), ischemic recurrence, and stent thrombosis. For CYP2C19 intermediate and poor metabolizers, CPIC recommends alternative antiplatelet agents such as prasugrel or ticagrelor that do not rely on CYP2C19 for activation.

## Guideline: CYP2D6 and Opioid Analgesics (Codeine and Tramadol)
Codeine (methylmorphine) is an opioid prodrug with very low intrinsic affinity for the mu-opioid receptor. To achieve clinically meaningful analgesia, codeine must undergo hepatic O-demethylation to its active metabolite morphine via CYP2D6. In CYP2D6 poor metabolizers (e.g., *4/*4, *5/*5, *4/*10), minimal morphine is generated, resulting in complete failure of pain relief and inadequate therapeutic response. Conversely, in ultra-rapid metabolizers who possess CYP2D6 gene duplications or multiplications (*1xN, *2xN), codeine is converted to morphine extremely rapidly and extensively. Even with standard therapeutic doses, ultra-rapid metabolizers experience high peak serum morphine concentrations, leading to severe opioid intoxication, profound central nervous system depression, respiratory arrest, and fatal toxicities. CPIC recommends avoiding codeine and tramadol in both poor and ultra-rapid metabolizers.
